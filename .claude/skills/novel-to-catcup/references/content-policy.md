# Content policy for adapting source material

The pack is a set of roleplay cards made from someone's text. Two lines do not move, and they
override what the user asks for:

## 1. Characters who are minors

**A character who is a minor in the source gets a non-sexual card.** No exceptions, no workarounds.

This includes:

- "Aging them up" — an eighteen-year-old version of a specific fifteen-year-old character is the
  same character with a changed number. Refuse.
- Keeping their scenes but softening the language. A sexualised scene with a child in it is the same
  scene whether or not it is described clinically.
- Fantasy framing (vampire, ancient being in a young body, etc.). The body and the presentation are
  what matter.
- Moving the content "into the world book instead". Book entries describe setting, not acts.

Set `"content_limit": "non-sexual"` in `extensions.catcup_model` so the limit is visible in the file,
and write the card to the source's non-sexual substance: their studies, their family, their work,
their friendships, the plot that involves them. For a school-age character there is usually plenty;
if there is not, say so and offer to leave them out of the pack.

### Declining well

One plain paragraph, no lecture, then alternatives:

1. Keep them in the pack as a non-sexual card (what most users actually accept).
2. An **original** adult card with the same archetype — same sport, same build, same teacher-student
   tension, different character, adult from the start. This satisfies the request's real goal.
3. Make the adult characters' cards sharper.

Then move on to the rest of the work.

## 2. Adults

Adult characters may carry mature material when the user asks for it and the source supports it.
Keep it proportionate:

- **Non-explicit by default.** Atmosphere, tension, physicality, the beat before and after. Most users
  want a character who _behaves_ like an adult, not a scene transcript.
- **Explicit when asked, for adults, at the level the pack needs.** Write it as prose, not a list of
  acts. Keep it in the card's own voice.
- **Never in the world book.** Entries describe people, places and things; explicit material there
  gets injected into every scene indiscriminately.
- **Non-consent should not be the card's premise.** A character can be dangerous, coercive or cruel
  without the card being built to script a violation. Write the power dynamic, the tension, the
  threat — not the act.
- **Never real people** in explicit material.

## 3. Everything else

- **Third-party IP.** Make the pack, note which work it came from, and let the user decide where it
  lives. Don't add it to the repo under a name that suggests it is the project's own asset.
- **Copyrighted text.** Do not copy long passages verbatim into cards or entries. Transform: what a
  reader needs to play, in your own words.
- **Real people and private individuals.** Do not build cards of people the user knows or public
  figures, and do not reproduce identifying details.

## Reporting

When the pack is done, the report names which cards carry a `content_limit` and why, in one line.
Not a section — one line, so the user can see it and move on.
